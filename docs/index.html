01 / OVERVIEW

The research question

A fair comparison of hybrid search—not simply a contest against weak baselines.

AABSTRACT / PROJECT SUMMARY

HBALS is a population-based metaheuristic for the Travelling Salesman Problem. It starts from a mixed set of nearest-neighbour and random tours, allocates search effort to elite and selected sites, and replaces the remaining population with scouts. Local improvement is applied selectively through first-improvement 2-opt and a lightweight segment-reversal operation.

The v2 study asks whether the Bees-style search contributes value when competing methods use the same 2-opt semantics and receive the same move-evaluation budget. It also tests which design components matter through a controlled ablation.

02 / PROBLEM

The Travelling Salesman Problem

A classic combinatorial optimisation problem with a simple objective and a difficult search space.

Given a set of cities and pairwise travel costs, the task is to find a minimum-length closed tour that visits every city exactly once and returns to its starting point.

The study uses TSPLIB EUC_2D instances. Distances are Euclidean and rounded to the nearest integer as specified by the TSPLIB convention used in the implementation.

WHAT THE TESTS SAY

Strong on the main set, not universally dominant.

The Friedman test detects an overall difference among the six methods on the 12 main instances. Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on instance means show HBALS significantly ahead of GA-2opt, ACO-2opt, MS-2opt and plain BA (adjusted p = 0.00244 for each). The HBALS–ILS-2opt comparison is not significant (adjusted p = 0.1294): HBALS has the lower mean gap on 9 instances, while ILS has the lower mean gap on 3.

The larger set adds context: ILS-2opt has lower mean gaps than HBALS on a280 and lin318. These results support a competitive outcome on the main benchmark set, not a claim of universal superiority.

COMPONENT ABLATION · 4 INSTANCES

Which parts of HBALS matter?

Mean percentage gap across st70, kroA100, ch130 and d198 · 20 runs per variant.

VariantMean gapΔ vs full

Descriptively, removing elite-site 2-opt raises mean gap from 0.275% to 0.818%; using adaptive neighbourhood raises it to 1.030%, and a neighbourhood size of three raises it to 1.687%. Removing selected-site 2-opt or segment reversal does not worsen the four-instance mean in this ablation. These are ablation-set observations, not a universal causal guarantee.

INTERPRETATION

Evidence with boundaries.

A good average rank is not proof that one method wins every instance. HBALS is competitive on the main group and shows a favourable mean gap, while ILS remains a statistically similar comparator and performs better on two of the three larger cases.
